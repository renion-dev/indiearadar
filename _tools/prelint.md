## What is Prelint?

**Prelint** (https://prelint.com) is a specialized developer tool designed to solve a growing problem in the era of generative AI: **product drift**. As developers increasingly rely on LLMs like GPT-4 or Claude to generate code, projects often lose consistency, deviate from architectural standards, or suffer from "hallucinated" best practices. Prelint acts as an intelligent guardrail, ensuring that AI-generated code remains aligned with your project’s specific style, security requirements, and technical constraints.

## Key Features

*   **Context-Aware Linting:** Unlike standard linters, Prelint understands the intent behind your codebase. It scans AI-suggested snippets to ensure they don't introduce logic that contradicts your existing patterns.
*   **Drift Detection:** It automatically flags code that deviates from your defined architecture or dependency requirements, preventing "spaghetti" AI code from polluting your repo.
*   **Custom Rule Sets:** Define your own project standards. Whether you want to enforce specific library usage or avoid certain anti-patterns, Prelint adapts to your team's unique needs.
*   **CI/CD Integration:** Seamlessly integrates into your existing workflow, acting as a final "sanity check" before code is merged.

## Why Indie Hackers Love It

For indie hackers and solo founders, time is the most valuable asset. While AI helps ship features faster, the "clean-up" phase—fixing buggy AI suggestions or refactoring inconsistent patterns—can be a massive time sink. Prelint is a favorite among the indie community because it automates the code review process, allowing solo builders to maintain high-quality codebases without needing a senior engineer looking over their shoulder. It effectively turns your AI assistant into a disciplined junior developer.

If you use AI to write code, **Prelint is an essential utility.** It bridges the gap between "fast code" and "maintainable code." By preventing product drift at the source, it saves countless hours of debugging and refactoring, making it a must-have tool for any modern software stack.
