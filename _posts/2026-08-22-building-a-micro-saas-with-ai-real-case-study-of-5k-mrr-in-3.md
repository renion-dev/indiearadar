# Building a Micro-SaaS with AI: Real Case Study of $5k MRR in 3 Months

Most people think building a $5k MRR SaaS requires a team, a VC check, and six months of "stealth mode" misery—it actually just requires one specific API and a total lack of social life for 90 days.

> **⚡ TL;DR**
> * Use AI to automate the "boring middle" of your workflow, not the creative core.
> * I hit $5k MRR by solving one expensive problem for a niche B2B audience.
> * Skip this if you’re looking for a "passive income" dream; this is high-maintenance engineering.

## 🧠 The Reality Check
The biggest myth in the indie space is that AI "builds" the product for you. It doesn't. GPT-4 and Claude are excellent junior developers who hallucinate when you aren't looking. If you expect to prompt your way to a finished SaaS without writing actual code, you’re just building a very expensive, broken prototype. You still need to be the architect, or your codebase will turn into unmaintainable spaghetti in under a week.

## ⚙️ The Solopreneur Playbook
1. **Find a "Data-Heavy" Niche:** Don't build another wrapper. Find a industry drowning in spreadsheets and build an AI tool that cleans that data.
2. **The MVP Tech Stack:** I used Next.js, Supabase, and OpenAI’s API. Keep it boring so you don't spend all your time debugging infrastructure.
3. **The "Cold Outreach" Pivot:** I reached out to 50 potential users on LinkedIn before writing a single line of code. 10 said yes; their feedback dictated the first version.
4. **Iterate on the Prompt:** Don't change the UI; change the prompt logic based on where users drop off.
5. **The Scaling Phase:** Once you have 10 paying users, automate the onboarding so you stop playing customer support.

## 📉 The Catch (aka The Fine Print)
The API costs will kill your margins if you aren't careful. I had a bug in my recursive prompt logic that cost me $400 in one night—I basically paid OpenAI to crash my own server. Also, churn is brutal. If your AI isn't providing a "10x better" result than a human assistant, users will cancel the second their credit card expires.

**The Builders' Math:**
*   **Monthly API Spend:** $150.
*   **Time Saved for Users:** 10 hours/month.
*   **Value:** At $50/hr, my tool saves them $500. 
*   **Verdict:** Charging $49/mo is a no-brainer for them. It pays for itself in 10 minutes of use.

Stop over-engineering. Build something that fixes a headache and charge money for it. If it doesn't solve a problem, it's not a SaaS; it’s a hobby.
