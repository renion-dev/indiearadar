# How I Built and Launched a High-Converting Site for $0 in 2028

Let’s be honest: the "indie hacker" dream often starts with spending $500 on a premium template, $50 on a domain you’ll never use, and three weeks tweaking a CSS shadow that no one will notice.

I’ve been there. But for my latest project, I decided to challenge myself: **zero dollars, zero bloat, and a launch in under 48 hours.** 

Here is how I built a high-converting landing page without spending a single cent.

## 1. The "Cheapskate" Tech Stack
Forget the expensive CMS or custom-coded React apps. If you want speed and conversion, keep it brutally simple. Here is the stack I used:

*   **Framework:** [Astro](https://astro.build/). It’s lightning-fast, handles static sites like a dream, and is free.
*   **Hosting:** [Vercel](https://vercel.com/) (Hobby Tier). It’s the gold standard for indie devs. Free, global CDN, and automated deployments.
*   **Design:** [Tailwind UI](https://tailwindui.com/) (Free components) + [Lucide Icons](https://lucide.dev/). Don’t reinvent the wheel; just steal the best practices.
*   **Form Handling:** [Formspree](https://formspree.io/). Their free tier is perfect for collecting early access signups without needing a backend.

**Pro-tip:** Spend your time writing copy, not choosing fonts. If your headline doesn't solve a pain point, a fancy background animation won't save you.

## 2. Copywriting Over Coding
The biggest mistake makers make is focusing on the "how" instead of the "why." I didn't write code until I had a one-sentence value proposition that my grandmother could understand.

I used the **PAS Framework** for the landing page:
*   **Problem:** Identify the pain point (e.g., "Tired of wasting hours on manual CSV cleaning?").
*   **Agitation:** Make it hurt a little (e.g., "You’re losing 5 hours every week to spreadsheet hell.").
*   **Solution:** Present your tool as the hero (e.g., "Automate your data workflow in 30 seconds with [Project Name].").

## 3. The "Zero-Budget" Launch
Once the site was live, I didn't spend money on ads. I went where the people were:
*   **Indie Hackers & Twitter/X:** I shared the *journey*, not just the link. People love a "building in public" story.
*   **Relevant Subreddits:** I found a specific sub for my niche, posted a genuine question, and mentioned the tool only when it was helpful.
*   **Product Hunt:** Use the free launch tools, but prepare your assets early. 

## The Bottom Line
Complexity is the enemy of profit. By keeping my budget at $0, I was forced to focus on the only thing that actually moves the needle: **solving a real problem for a real person.**

Stop tinkering with your favicon and go ship that landing page. Your users are waiting.
