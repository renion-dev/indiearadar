# Building a Micro-SaaS with AI: Real Case Study of $5k MRR in 3 Months

Most "AI-powered" SaaS founders are just wrapping a thin UI around a prompt and praying for retention.

> **⚡ TL;DR**
> * I hit $5k MRR in 90 days by automating a boring B2B workflow with GPT-4o.
> * The secret isn't the AI—it’s the specific, tedious niche I chose to solve.
> * Skip this if you think AI will write your entire codebase or magically find your product-market fit.

## 🧠 The Reality Check
Everyone thinks building an AI SaaS means creating a "smarter" version of ChatGPT. That is a graveyard of abandoned GitHub repos. The real money isn't in "intelligence"; it’s in "integration." Stop trying to build a chatbot and start building a tool that does one specific, annoying thing that currently requires a human to copy-paste between three different browser tabs.

## ⚙️ The Solopreneur Playbook
1. **Find the friction:** Scrape Reddit or LinkedIn for people complaining about a specific, manual data-entry task.
2. **Build the pipe:** Use Vercel + Supabase to connect the source data to an OpenAI API endpoint.
3. **Ignore the UI:** Build a bare-bones frontend; focus 90% of your energy on the prompt engineering for edge cases.
4. **Cold outreach:** Don't use ads. DM the people complaining in step 1 and offer a 7-day trial.
5. **Iterate on feedback:** Fix the specific output errors your users report immediately.

## 📉 The Catch (aka The Fine Print)
The prompt engineering is a nightmare. You will spend 40 hours tweaking a system prompt only for OpenAI to update their model and break your entire logic. I broke the production server twice last month trying to optimize context windows. Also, your margins are at the mercy of API pricing. If your prompt gets too verbose, you’ll burn your profit margin in a single afternoon of high traffic.

## The Builders' Math
*   **Monthly API Cost:** $150.
*   **Time Saved for Users:** 10 hours/week.
*   **Value:** If their time is worth $30/hr, you save them $1,200/month.
*   **Result:** Charging $49/mo is a no-brainer. It pays off in less than 1 hour of usage.

Stop over-engineering. Build the bridge between two broken systems, slap a subscription button on it, and see if anyone reaches for their wallet. If they don't, kill it and move to the next idea by Friday.
