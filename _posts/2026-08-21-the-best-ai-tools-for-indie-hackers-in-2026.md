If your AI stack costs more than your hosting bill, you aren’t a solopreneur; you’re just a venture-backed charity for software giants.

> **⚡ TL;DR**
> * **Cursor** is the only IDE that actually writes production code without hallucinating constant syntax errors.
> * **Perplexity Pro** replaced my Google Search and my expensive research assistant. 
> * **Skip this if** you are still building "wrappers" that add nothing but a fancy UI to GPT-4o.

## 🧠 The Reality Check
Everyone thinks AI will write their entire app while they sip coffee. It won't. AI is a junior developer who works for pennies but requires constant supervision. If you don't know how to code, AI won't make you a founder; it will make you a victim of spaghetti code you can’t debug.

## ⚙️ The Solopreneur Playbook
1. Use **Cursor** to index your entire codebase for context-aware refactoring.
2. Draft your feature specs in a plain text file before asking the AI to touch a single line of code.
3. Paste the spec into Cursor’s Composer mode to generate the boilerplate in one go.
4. Run your test suite immediately; never trust the AI to write bug-free logic on the first try.
5. Use **Perplexity** to find specific API documentation or library edge cases that LLMs usually get wrong.

## 📉 The Catch
Cursor is phenomenal until it decides to rewrite your entire project architecture because of a vague prompt. You have to babysit the diffs. If you blindly hit "Accept All," you *will* break your production server. I learned this the hard way at 3 AM on a Tuesday. Also, the subscription costs are starting to stack up like a bad habit.

## The Builders' Math
* **Cost:** $20/mo (Cursor Pro). 
* **Time Saved:** 5 hours/week on boilerplate and debugging. 
* **Value:** At a modest $50/hr billing rate, you’ve gained $1,000 of value for $20. 
* **ROI:** It pays for itself in about 30 minutes of work.

Stop chasing the "AI wrapper of the week." Pick a stack that handles the boring stuff so you can get back to building features that people actually pay for. If you find yourself spending more time prompting than deploying, you’ve already lost.
