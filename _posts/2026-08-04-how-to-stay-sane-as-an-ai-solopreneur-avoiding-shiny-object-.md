Most AI solopreneurs are currently burning out because they’re chasing the "next big wrapper" instead of building a product that actually solves a problem.

> **⚡ TL;DR**
> *   Stop testing every new LLM model; pick one stack and stick to it for 30 days.
> *   Focus on "boring" automation rather than flashy AI agents that hallucinate.
> *   Skip this if you enjoy the dopamine hit of tinkering more than shipping code.

## 🧠 The Reality Check
The biggest myth in the indie space is that adding "AI-powered" to your landing page instantly attracts paying users. It doesn’t. Users don’t care if you use Claude, GPT-4, or a bunch of nested `if` statements. They care if your tool saves them time or makes them money. If you spend your morning refreshing Twitter for the latest API update, you aren’t a founder—you’re a consumer.

## ⚙️ The Solopreneur Playbook
1.  **Define your "One-Tool" Stack:** Choose one LLM and one vector database. Ignore everything else until you hit your first $1k MRR.
2.  **Kill the "What-If" Folder:** Delete your bookmarks of "promising" tools you haven't touched in a week.
3.  **Build the Boring Core:** Automate your most repetitive task first, even if it’s just a simple script.
4.  **Ship, Then Optimize:** Use basic prompts until your users complain about the output quality.

**Builders' Math:**
*   Cost of "Shiny Object" subscription: $40/mo.
*   Time lost context-switching: 5 hours/week (20 hours/mo).
*   At a $50/hr opportunity cost, you’re losing $1,040/mo chasing hype.
*   Verdict: Stop playing and start building.

## 📉 The Catch
The catch is that this approach is incredibly boring. You won’t get the rush of being the first to deploy an experimental agent. You will feel behind when someone posts a viral demo of a tool you ignored. You have to be okay with being "unfashionable" while your bank account grows. Also, you *will* eventually need to upgrade your stack, but do it when your product breaks, not because a VC-funded startup released a new library on GitHub. 

I spent three days last month integrating a "cutting-edge" agent framework only to realize it was slower and more expensive than a basic API call. I broke my production environment twice for zero gain. Don't be me. Build, ship, and ignore the noise.
