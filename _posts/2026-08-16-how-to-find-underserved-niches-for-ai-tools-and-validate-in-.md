Building an AI wrapper that solves a problem nobody actually has is the fastest way to burn your runway and your sanity.

> **⚡ TL;DR**
> *   Stop building for "everyone"; look for "workflow-stuck" cohorts on niche subreddits.
> *   Validate with a landing page + waitlist before writing a single line of production code.
> *   Skip this if you’re looking for a "get rich quick" viral hit; this is for boring, profitable software.

## 🧠 The Reality Check
The biggest myth in the indie space is that you need a "unique" AI idea. You don't. You need a boring, painful workflow that people are currently solving with a spreadsheet and a prayer. If you’re trying to build the next "AI for everyone," you’ve already lost. Focus on the guy manually formatting legal documents at 2 AM. That’s where the money hides.

## ⚙️ The Solopreneur Playbook
1.  **Find the Pain:** Search Reddit/Twitter for "How do I automate [boring task]?" or "Excel formula for [specific misery]."
2.  **Verify the Volume:** Look for at least 50 recent posts asking for the same solution.
3.  **The "Fake Door" Landing Page:** Spin up a Carrd site in 2 hours describing the solution as if it exists.
4.  **Run the Traffic:** Drop the link in those specific threads with a "Hey, I'm building this, check it out" note.
5.  **Measure Intent:** If nobody clicks the "Join Waitlist" button, the idea is trash. Move on immediately.

## 📉 The Catch
The fine print is that you will feel like a fraud. You aren't building a "real" product yet, just a promise. You’ll also get hate from purists who think building an AI wrapper is "lazy." Let them stay broke. The real risk is spending two weeks coding a feature that nobody wants to pay for. I once spent 40 hours on a Chrome extension that solved a problem I *thought* was urgent; I got exactly zero signups. Don't be me.

**The Builders' Math:**
*   Cost: $20 (Domain + Carrd). 
*   Time: 4 hours. 
*   Result: 15 emails. 
*   At $50/mo subscription value, you’ve validated $750/mo potential revenue in a single afternoon.

If the waitlist stays empty, kill the idea. Don’t fall in love with your code. Fall in love with the data that tells you what to build next.
